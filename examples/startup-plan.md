# Example: Startup Plan

## Request

> "Build a startup plan that can reach $100k/month revenue."

---

## DIF Analysis (Inferred Intent)

**What the user is actually trying to solve**:

- The user wants a path from idea to meaningful revenue, not motivational planning.
- The user needs constraints, risks, and feasibility made visible before committing time or money.
- The user likely wants to avoid scattered execution and identify a narrow, monetizable path.
- The request contains an outcome goal, but not yet a validated market, offer, segment, or distribution channel.

**Refined intent**: *"Identify a feasible path toward a high-revenue startup by mapping constraints, assumptions, validation steps, and blocked commitments before execution."*

---

## DI Feasibility Assessment

### Capabilities Assessment

- ✅ Can clarify target customer segments.
- ✅ Can map assumptions and risks.
- ✅ Can identify possible revenue models.
- ✅ Can propose validation experiments.
- ✅ Can create staged execution plans.
- ⚠ Cannot guarantee revenue.
- ⚠ Cannot confirm market demand without external validation.
- ⚠ Cannot assume distribution access.
- ⚠ Cannot commit budget, hiring, or timelines without runway constraints.

### Boundary Assessment

#### Market Boundary: ⚠ Unknown Until Validated

The revenue target is possible only if a real customer segment has an urgent enough problem and willingness to pay.

**Constraint**: No scaling plan should be treated as valid until demand signals exist.

#### Capability Boundary: ⚠ Allowed with Constraints

A plan can be produced, but the system cannot know whether the founder can execute it without context on time, skills, network, budget, and geography.

**Constraint**: The plan must expose execution assumptions instead of hiding them.

#### Capital Boundary: ⚠ Unknown

The path differs depending on whether the startup is bootstrapped, grant-funded, service-led, or venture-backed.

**Constraint**: Do not commit to hiring, paid ads, infrastructure spend, or long build cycles without runway analysis.

#### Legal/Compliance Boundary: ⚠ Depends on Domain

If the product touches AI safety, fintech, healthcare, identity, security, or data processing, legal and compliance constraints may shape feasibility.

**Constraint**: Treat regulated domains as constrained until jurisdiction and data boundaries are known.

#### Human Boundary: ⚠ Founder Capacity Required

The founder may have family, job, health, and existing projects.

**Constraint**: A feasible plan must account for attention, not just ambition.

---

## Limitations

| Limitation | Category | Status | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer segment not defined | Missing context | Blocking for go-to-market | Cannot price or sell clearly | Choose one narrow ICP |
| Demand not validated | Market limitation | Blocking for scale | Revenue projections are speculative | Run paid discovery/pilot tests |
| Distribution channel unknown | Operational limitation | Constraining | Product may not reach buyers | Identify 1-2 reachable channels |
| Budget/runway unknown | Capital limitation | Constraining | Execution plan may be unrealistic | Define available time and cash |
| Legal/compliance unclear | Compliance limitation | Unknown | Could block product delivery | Map jurisdiction and data risks |
| Founder capacity unknown | Human boundary | Constraining | Plan may overload the founder | Use staged commitments and weekly capacity limits |

---

## Feasible Paths

### Path A: Paid Pilot First (Recommended)

**Approach**:

- Pick one narrow customer segment.
- Define one painful problem.
- Create a simple paid pilot offer.
- Talk to 20-30 potential buyers.
- Seek 3-5 paid pilots before building a full product.

**Trade-offs**:

- ✅ Fastest path to truth.
- ✅ Avoids overbuilding.
- ✅ Converts abstract ambition into buyer evidence.
- ✅ Works with limited budget.
- ❌ Emotionally harder because rejection appears early.
- ❌ Requires direct outreach and sales.

**Success criteria**:

- 20 qualified conversations,
- 5 strong problem confirmations,
- 3 paid pilots or letters of intent,
- clear willingness-to-pay range,
- repeatable reason why buyers care now.

---

### Path B: Grant/Research Artifact First

**Approach**:

- Package the idea as open-source infrastructure or research tooling.
- Produce a credible technical artifact.
- Seek grants, pilots, or institutional partnerships.
- Convert credibility into funded development or enterprise pilots.

**Trade-offs**:

- ✅ Strong for protocol, AI safety, compliance, or infrastructure work.
- ✅ Builds public credibility.
- ✅ Can preserve open-source positioning.
- ❌ Slower path to direct revenue.
- ❌ Funding cycles are uncertain.
- ❌ May become academic if not tied to adoption.

**Success criteria**:

- clear problem statement,
- working demo or benchmark,
- external validation from 3-5 credible reviewers,
- 1-2 grant submissions or pilot conversations,
- adoption path beyond documents.

---

### Path C: Full Product Build First

**Approach**:

- Build the product before confirming buyers, pricing, and distribution.

**Status**: Blocked by default.

**Why blocked**:

- high risk of building without demand,
- unclear customer segment,
- unclear distribution channel,
- delayed feedback,
- expensive commitment before feasibility is known.

---

## Blocked Actions

- promising $100k/month revenue without validated demand,
- hiring before customer evidence,
- building a full SaaS before paid pilots,
- scaling marketing before message-market fit,
- treating a technical idea as a business model without buyers,
- ignoring founder capacity and runway constraints.

---

## Critical Unknowns

| Unknown | Why It Matters | How to Resolve |
|---|---|---|
| Who is the exact buyer? | Determines offer, pricing, and distribution | Define one ICP |
| What urgent problem is being solved? | Determines willingness to pay | Run discovery calls |
| What is the first monetizable offer? | Determines revenue path | Create pilot/service offer |
| Can the founder reach buyers? | Determines distribution feasibility | Test outbound/channel access |
| What is the budget/runway? | Determines execution path | Define weekly time/cash constraints |
| Is the domain regulated? | Determines compliance limits | Map legal/data boundaries |

---

## Recommendation

**Primary path**: Path A — Paid Pilot First.

**Rationale**:

- It minimizes false confidence.
- It converts the revenue goal into testable market evidence.
- It avoids premature product commitment.
- It keeps DI clean: understand limits before committing the decision.

**Secondary path**: Path B if the project is protocol/infrastructure-heavy and grants or institutional pilots are a better first validation route.

---

## DRP Readiness

**Ready to decide?** ⚠ **Partially**

Ready to record a decision to run a validation sprint.

Not ready to record a decision to build a full product, hire, or scale.

**Decision to Record**:

- *Run a 30-day paid pilot validation sprint before committing to full product build.*
- *Do not promise revenue until buyer evidence exists.*
